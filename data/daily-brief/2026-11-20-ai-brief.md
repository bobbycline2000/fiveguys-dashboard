The three-item constraint forces prioritization instead of a firehose of observations.

## 4. Gotcha of the Week

**The trap:** You ask Claude "What should my food cost be?" and Claude gives you a confident answer: "Industry standard is 28–30%." You take it as gospel.

**What you missed:** Claude has never seen your P&L. It doesn't know your menu mix, your waste patterns, your supplier contracts, your store-level quirks. It knows the industry average and assumes you fit it. You don't.

**The fix:** Always give Claude YOUR data first. "My average food cost is 31%. My check average is $12.50. My traffic is 850 customers a day. My biggest cost driver is [whatever]. Given that, what should I be targeting?" Now Claude has constraints. Its answer becomes useful instead of generic.
